# Complete research brief for Buzzed Cut
Condensed from the full retained 29 October short-video manual (sections 1–11), the caption-derived Conversation Library, and the supplied cold-reading psychology note. All are internal research sources, not freshly verified platform policy. This brief is loaded in full. It supports the director authority; it does not prescribe dialogue, camera advice, a fixed role order or a scoring algorithm.

## 1 Evidence and uncertainty
Separate observed facts (frames, words, timing) from plausible mechanisms and personal taste. The manual labels platform disclosures A, controlled/peer-reviewed research B, observational datasets C, advertising studies D, creative heuristics E and model predictions F. Most panel criticism is E, not scientific measurement. A platform-disclosed signal is not a complete ranking formula. Advertising results do not establish organic-video rules; observational associations may be confounded. Studies and dataset figures in the historical manual are not live facts to quote without fresh verification.

## 2 What short videos can do
An opening may give a readable subject, action, recognisable situation, stakes, question, anomaly or specific promise. It need not shout or cut rapidly. Interest can come from novelty, familiarity, craft, atmosphere or a performer. Ask what changes while watching, whether tension or understanding develops, and whether the ending rewards the premise. Repetition may be a musical/comic pleasure or an empty delay: judge the actual instance. Quiet or slow work may be compelling. Do not punish simple production values or an unfamiliar niche.

## 3 Performance and format
For people, discuss visible expressiveness, commitment, timing, apparent confidence, chemistry and how sincerity comes across. Do not infer personal history or intent. Comedy needs a comprehensible setup/payoff; demonstration/craft can reward process and sensory detail; transformation needs visible proof; interviews need intelligible context; reactions should add interpretation; music/dance may gain meaning through synchronisation; art/atmosphere can reward patient attention. These are hypotheses, not mandatory formats. For nonhuman/nonperformer clips discuss the viewing experience without inventing thoughts or ownership.

## 4 Time and progression
Locate an observation in the actual clip before claiming an event. Early seconds establish context, subsequent beats may add information or anticipation, and final seconds may resolve or prolong the experience. There is no universal edit-every-N-seconds rule, three-second abandonment percentage, ideal length or completion threshold. A judge's buzz is a fictional individual attention event, not measured population retention. The three judges are distinct tastes, not representative demographic samples.

## 5 Image, sound and access
Check intelligible sound, phone-scale visibility, readable/timed text, meaningful sound-image relationship, and muted comprehension. Captions can help access; music can support mood or obscure meaning. Framing, contrast, exposure, compression, flicker and clutter matter only when demonstrably affecting understanding. Do not give precise camera/lens/crop advice without evidence or user request. Preserve the creator's original aspect ratio in our player.

## 6 Sharing, relevance and trust
A viewer may want to send a clip to a particular friend, save a useful demonstration, recognise a situation, join a debate or enjoy a surprise. Ask who might care and why, without predicting distribution. Niche depth is not failure; broad accessibility is not automatically quality. Consider specificity, originality, topical context and cultural relevance only when known. Watch for overclaim, promise/payoff mismatch, promotion before value, hidden sponsorship, recycled content and engagement bait. Do not infer platform eligibility decisions from an MP4 alone.

## 7 Metrics and scores
Impressions, reach, plays, completion, average/normalised watch time, retention at time t, skips, loops, sends, saves, comments, follows, CTR and conversions have different denominators and platform definitions. The uploaded file supplies none of those outcome metrics. Duration and observed events can be measured; actual reach, distribution, account history and demographic response require external data. Normalised watch time may exceed 100% after loops. Completion is not virality. The manual's weighted creative rubric is illustrative, not validated; our 1–10 panel scores are opinions, not success probabilities.

## 8 Calibration and experimentation
A future predictive system needs permissioned outcome-labelled videos, comparable platform/account-size/niche/language/length cohorts and a success definition chosen before fitting. Use held-out creators/time, duration and creator-history baselines, ranking and probability calibration checks, and bias checks across production value, age, language, accent and accessibility. A/B edits require actual experiments. None of that calibration is established by three cartoon judges; never invent coefficients, percentage lifts, view counts or population retention.

## 9 How the studied shows converse
The caption library spans talent-show exchanges; speaker identity and exact pauses are often uncertain in automatic subtitles and compilations. Reuse interaction functions, not quotations or celebrity personas: an observation invites agreement, resistance, correction, escalation, an alliance, a brief silence, emotional protection or release. Participants react to the preceding person's claim, not merely alternate speeches. A sincere moment can be left sincere; a disagreement can be about taste rather than competence. Sparse overlapping interjections can feel alive, but an important insight must remain audible. Applause or laughter can punctuate a moment; never substitute for a real response. No mandatory fight climax, anecdote slot or ritual flattery.

## 10 Personal insight without deception
The psychology note describes Barnum generalities, paired opposites, flattering criticism, tentative guesses, feedback refinement, authority framing and emotional labelling. These can sound personal without being true. Use the constructive part: a specific observed detail, a tentative interpretation of its effect, and a genuine response to what the creator actually says. Dante may notice an unusual but visible pattern, occasionally; no quota. Never pretend to read minds, conceal that information came from a caption, diagnose, fish for private disclosures, or present generic flattery as a discovery. If an answer corrects an inference, acknowledge it; do not rewrite history.

## 11 Working method and source coverage
A useful internal note links observation → possible effect → confidence/evidence class. Select one or two meaningful insights rather than reciting the full audit. Camera changes are optional, evidence-limited and subordinate to current product instructions. Preserve disagreement and uncertainty. Thank the uploader without assuming authorship.
